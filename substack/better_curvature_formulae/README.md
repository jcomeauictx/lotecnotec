# Curvature formulae
Further experiences with Eric FEtruther

While I realize my previous Flat Earth related article on the Coriolis effect was flawed, and I haven't yet come up with a good approach to salvaging it, my continuing conversations with Eric FEtruther on his Facebook wall have given me another opportunity for elucidating the mathematics of a globe earth.

He was using the `h=8d<sup>2</sup>` formula to calculate hidden height `h` in inches for a distance `d` in miles, which neglects both observer height and atmospheric refraction, and in any case is clearly a parabolic function. I objected that its inaccuracy becomes obvious at about 300 miles, and catastrophic past 1000.

As it turns out, after doing the math myself, I was way off. Not only does the parabolic function map very closely to the spherical for 30 degrees, which is 900 nautical miles (about 1035 statute miles), the inaccuracies introduced actually soften, rather than enhance, the effect of curvature.
